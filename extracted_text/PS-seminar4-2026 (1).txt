(d) Are random variables X and Y independent?

Problem 3 (Poisson distribution). Suppose that the number of new COVID-19 positive tested
patients in the city A on day i is a Poisson random variable Xi of parameter λ = 10; assume
also that the random variables X1 , X2 , . . . are independent.
(a) Find the probability that the number of newly COVID-19 infected patients tomorrow will
be larger than today’s count.
Assume that the number of new COVID-19 positive tested patients in the city B on day i is
a Poisson random variable Yi of parameter µ = 20; assume also that all random variables Xi
and Yj , i, j = 1, 2, . . . are independent.
(b) Show that the random variable Xi + Yi has Poisson distribution of parameter 30. In the
general case, if X and Y are independent Poisson random variables with parameters λ and
µ respectively, what is the distribution of the random variable X + Y ?
(c) Assume that by this noon, 5 new cases were reported. Find the probability that k newly
COVID-19 infected patients will be reported today in total.
Hint: Can you see that there are two ways to solve this problem, depending on whether we make some
assumptions about the distribution of cases throughout the day? What assumptions can be made?

(d) Assume that 30 new COVID-19 infected patients were reported yesterday in the cities A
and B in total. What is the probability that k of them come from A, k = 0, 1, . . . , 30?
Problem 4 (Binomial distribution). A communications channel transmits the digits 0 and
1. However, due to noise, the digit transmitted is incorrectly received with probability 0.2.
Suppose that we want to transmit an important message consisting of n binary digits. To
reduce the chance of error, we transmit 00000 instead of 0 and 11111 instead of 1. If the
receiver of the message uses the “majority” rule for decoding, what is the probability that
(a) the message will be wrong when decoded?
(b) at most two binary digits of the original message will be wrongly decoded?
(c) what is the most probable number of correctly decoded binary digits?
What independence assumptions are you making?

Problem 6 (Binomial distribution). When coin A is flipped, it lands on Heads with probability
0.4; when coin B is flipped, it lands on Heads with probability 0.6. One of these coins is
randomly chosen and flipped 10 times; let X denote the total number of Heads.
(a) Is it true that a r.v. X has a binomial distribution?
(b) Find the p.m.f. of the random variable X.
(c) Given that the first of these ten flips lands Heads, what is the conditional distribution of
the total number of Heads? (Hint: condition on the coin chosen)
(d) Assuming that X = k, what is the (conditional) probability that coin A is tossed? For
what k is it larger than 0.5?
Problem 7 (Binomial distribution). Suppose that a biased coin that lands on Heads with
probability p is flipped 10 times. Given that a total of 6 Heads results, find the conditional
probability that the first 3 outcomes
(a) are HTT (meaning that the first flip results in Heads, the second in Tails, and the third in
Tails);
(b) include precisely k Heads; k = 0, 1, 2, 3.
Assume now that there are 2 Heads in the first three tosses of the coin.
(c) What is the conditional probability that there are k Heads in all 10 tosses?
(d) What is the most probable number of Heads in all 10 tosses?
Problem 8 (Joint probability distribution ). You roll two fair dice, and they show X and Y ,
respectively. Let U = min{X, Y }, V = max{X, Y }.
(a) Write down the joint distributions of:
(i) {U, X};

(ii) {U, V };

(iii) {X, Y, V }

(b) Which of the above r.v. are independent?
(c) Find the conditional p.m.f. of the r.v. X | U = u and Y | V = v.
