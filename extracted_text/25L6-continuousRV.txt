Independence
Definition
R.v.’s X and Y are called independent if for all x and y the events
{X ≤ x} and {Y ≤ y } are independent.
Thus for independent X and Y
P(X ≤ x, Y ≤ y ) = P(X ≤ x) · P(Y ≤ y );
or, in terms of cumulative distribution functions (c.d.f.)
FX ,Y (x, y ) = FX (x)FY (y)
or, in terms of probability density functions (p.d.f.),
fX ,Y (x, y) = fX (x) · fY (y).
Remark
The notion of independence is extended in a natural way to any number
of random variables.
